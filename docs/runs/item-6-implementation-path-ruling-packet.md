## Item 6 — Implementation: extend `daedalus.mjs` + orchestrator vs workshop-first proof

### What is actually at stake

The v2 design doc's own "Mapping to `C:\Users\dsmce\telos`" table (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-20-daedalus-workflow-v2-design.md:721-732`) says Stage 1 (`runParallelDaedalus`, `build-gate/daedalus.mjs:311`) and hash/order (`compileAndHashPlan`, `merkle-dag/planner.mjs:42`) already exist and are wired into `build-gate/proposal-orchestrator.mjs:32,35,210,242`; what v2 would newly require is Stage 0 (research pre-flight), full Stage 2/4 challenge-as-gate-plus-deny-back (today "Grok often advisory"), and the referee wired over a produce loop. Item 6 asks how that delta gets built: by extending the shipped `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` in place under a fully-specified, committee-authorized plan before any of it runs once, or by proving the mechanics in a disposable workshop first and only then writing a scoped extension.

This is not an abstract choice. A closely related question was put to a real test in this repo, one day after v2 was HELD, under a closely related name: the **Daedalus Family Multi-Model Seat Lifecycle** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-21-daedalus-family-multi-model-seat-lifecycle-design.md`, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-21-daedalus-family-multi-model-seat-lifecycle.md`). It targets a different package (`multi-model-seats`, not `build-gate/daedalus.mjs` directly), and — on inspection — it is not a clean single-variable match for "the same problem class." Its own Task list (Task 1 "Canonical Hashing, Closed Typed Artifacts, and Baseline Audit," Task 3 "Signed Crash-Atomic Merkle-DAG Store," Task 5 "Disk-Only Replay," Task 8 "Resumable Serialized Production Controller and CLI," Task 9 "Closed Detached-Verifiable Handoff Bundle") builds an entire new trust-primitive stack from scratch in a standalone package — its own hashing, its own signed DAG store, its own controller/CLI, its own replay/handoff machinery — none of it reusing TELOS's already-reviewed `merkle-dag`/`build-gate` infrastructure. That is a real confound: the plan may have failed because it reinvented trust primitives from scratch, not (or not only) because it was planned mega-scale-first. What it does share with v2's Stage 0/2/4 delta is scale and rigor of specification (pair-produce gates, cold-review gates, signed Merkle-DAG decision nodes, Eye authority, no-cap pass-or-return), authorized as a single mega-plan before Task 1 starts. It was attempted five times (`authz-009` through the in-progress `authz-013`) and refused four straight times. In parallel, and unrelated to that authorization track, a real workshop prototype of v2's Stage 0/2/referee mechanics (`docs/runs/production-profile-compiler-1-workshop/daedalus-v2.mjs`) was built, tested (35/35 passing), and cited by exact line number in the item-7 ruling packet — without a single council round.

There is also a direct counter-precedent this packet must weigh, and did not check in its first draft: commit `2e530b2` ("Wire parallel authorship into the proposal orchestrator") landed comparable dual-seat friction machinery — parallel authorship, convergence/conflict routing to The Eye — directly into `build-gate/daedalus.mjs` and `proposal-orchestrator.mjs` via an ordinary PR (#99), and `df65e8e` ("Harden the parallel-authorship path: close five fail-open gaps from cold review") then hardened it in response to a normal review pass, not a 5-model Eye-authorization council. That is on-point, on these exact files, and it succeeded. Item 6 is not asking a question this repo already ran as a clean natural experiment isolating "mode of proceeding" — it has suggestive, and partly conflicting, evidence, weighed below.

### Option A — extend `daedalus.mjs` + `proposal-orchestrator.mjs` in place, via a fully-specified authorized plan

**The case for it.** A single coherent, committee-reviewed plan against the real files means no throwaway code to discard, no later "which prototype pieces do we actually keep" step, and the full DAG/authority/signing machinery lands with the production-grade rigor `build-gate/` already requires (`CLAUDE.md:129-149`: gate must re-read disk ground truth, secrets never enter the repo, fail closed). If it clears review, Stage 0/2/4 arrive already meeting the bar the rest of `build-gate/` meets.

**The case against — what actually happened when this mode was run for real.** The `daedalus-family-lifecycle` plan shares the mega-plan-first, fully-specified-before-any-code-runs mode, aimed at a comparable class of governance mechanics — though, as noted above, it also reinvents its entire trust-primitive stack from scratch rather than reusing TELOS's existing `merkle-dag`/`build-gate` infrastructure, a confound this section's evidence does not by itself rule out. With that caveat, its trajectory is still informative:

- The design doc (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-21-daedalus-family-multi-model-seat-lifecycle-design.md`) grew from 268 lines (commit `f7482da`) into a plan of 934 lines (`881faac`), then +938/-176 lines to a "hardened" candidate (`c3fd3af`), then +1642/-307 lines to 3033 lines total (`f2db463`) — each "harden" pass in direct response to the previous round's council refusal, never in response to running code.
- The council-authorization runner script whose *only job is to certify and submit the plan document* — not to implement anything — grew from 334 lines (`docs/runs/daedalus-family-lifecycle-authorization-1/run-authorization.mjs`) to 3590 lines plus a dedicated 2990-line test suite (`docs/runs/daedalus-family-lifecycle-authorization-5/run-authorization.mjs`, `test-runner.mjs`, committed as `585e8bd "Prepare authz-013 council runner"`). That is more code spent proving the *cover letter* is trustworthy than exists in the entire v2 workshop prototype.
- Four concluded attempts, four refusals:
  - **authz-009** (`docs/runs/daedalus-family-lifecycle-authorization-1/authorization-summary.json:101-109`): blocked — codex `revise` with hard stops that the plan cannot re-derive state from disk, that `sha256Ref` is unsound (raw bytes vs canonical JSON means `"null"` and `null` collide), that recovery/mutation semantics can violate the plan's own invalidation rules, and that Eye authority and required-seat provenance are not actually authenticated by the proposed verifier.
  - **authz-010** (`.../authorization-2/authorization-summary.json:92-97`): blocked on transport failure alone (codex `ECONNRESET`) — no technical content, just infrastructure death, itself evidence of how much surface area the review call now has to hold.
  - **authz-011** (`.../authorization-3/authorization-summary.json:81-91`): blocked — claude, codex, and grok all `socket hang up`; only gemini and the local `agy` checkpoint returned.
  - **authz-012** (`.../authorization-4/authorization-summary.json:121-124`): blocked — codex `revise` again, and the hard stops are *new*, not repeats: the recovery stage can't represent its own failure ("Task 4 requires every recovery decision to be a pass" while the profile also routes recovery non-passes back to recovery); several of the plan's own mandated tests are provably unimplementable against the interfaces the plan itself specifies (a stateless validator can't detect duplicate journal indices; `verifyEyeAuthorityStatic` never receives the node it's asked to validate); the handoff API can't get the `outDir` its own contract requires; and — the sharpest single finding — **the plan's own final-verification section requires running its own credential-value detector over the complete plan and getting zero matches, but the detector as specified would reject the plan's own test literal** (`Bearer abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz`, present in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-21-daedalus-family-multi-model-seat-lifecycle.md`'s own recipe). A specification that cannot pass its own acceptance check against its own text is not a spec that has merely a bug; it has exceeded the complexity at which anyone — model or human — can hold its self-consistency in view.
  - **authz-013** is in preparation (`docs/runs/daedalus-family-lifecycle-authorization-5/`) but has not yet run a single seat.
- Net result after four real rounds and roughly 18 hours of wall-clock effort: zero lines of implementation code exist anywhere in `/home/colchis/plugins/multi-model-seats`. Every artifact produced is plan prose, review transcripts, or the runner script that submits plan prose for review.

**What it costs.** Five real council invocations (real provider calls across claude/codex/grok/gemini/agy, several of which died mid-call) spent certifying a document, not exercising code; and, if this mode is used to extend `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` directly, those are gate-adjacent files in the trust spine (`CLAUDE.md:60-83`) — a design this baroque, applied directly against files the merge gate depends on, is asking reviewers to certify an unrun system's self-consistency at a scale they have already twice failed to hold, and a scale where the plan's own credential gate cannot certify the plan's own text.

**What breaks if it is wrong.** If The Eye authorizes an extend-in-place mega-plan for Stage 0/2/4 against `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` and the same failure shape recurs — contradictions surfacing only on the *n*th hardening round, not the first — the failure domain is no longer "a review round costs an afternoon." It is a bug in the orchestrator the build gate actually calls, discovered after authorization, in code that was never run once before being merged.

### Option B — workshop-first proof, then a scoped extension plan

**The case for it.** This already happened, cheaply, for exactly the mechanics item 6 is asking about. `docs/runs/production-profile-compiler-1-workshop/daedalus-v2.mjs` (731 lines) implements Stage 0 research dispositions, Stage 2 challenge with denied-twice/oscillation/cap terminals, Seat 5 process checkpoints, and the role-R referee — explicitly *without* modifying the shipped Stage 1 join: "Stage 1 ... is the existing `runWorkshop` parallel join in `workshop-lib.mjs` and is deliberately NOT modified here; `runDaedalusV2` composes around it through an injected `runJoin`" (`daedalus-v2.mjs:6-8`). Its referee implementation (`readRefereeVerdict`, `daedalus-v2.mjs:298-330`; call site `:583-611`) is the exact code the item-7 ruling packet cites by line number as already-built-and-tested evidence. Running its test suite live confirms it: `node --test test-daedalus-v2.mjs` passes all 35 cases (denied-twice terminals, oscillation, cap-as-ceiling, referee stalemate at round ≥ 2, provenance burn on reused response ids, transport-failure-burns-not-crashes, and more), in 903 lines of tests against 731 lines of implementation — smaller than a single bullet of the family-lifecycle plan's Global Constraints section. It required zero council rounds, zero Eye rulings, and left `build-gate/daedalus.mjs` (351 lines, last touched by `2017822 "Seat-keyed model/prompt data"`) completely untouched throughout.

**The case against.** The workshop directory is currently untracked (`git status`: `?? docs/runs/production-profile-compiler-1-workshop/`) — it is proof-of-concept evidence, not shipped or reviewed code, and it only covers the plan side (Stage 0/2); Stage 3/4 (code produce/challenge) has no workshop evidence yet at all. "Prove it in a workshop" does not by itself resolve *which* prototype pieces are safe to promote into the trust-sensitive orchestrator — that promotion step still needs real rigor, just applied to a smaller, evidence-backed diff instead of an unrun 3033-line mega-spec. There is also a standing risk of prototype creep: at least fourteen `clotho-daedalus-delta*` workshop directories already exist under `docs/runs/` without a stated graduation path, so "workshop-first" needs its own exit criterion or it becomes permanent shadow infrastructure that never feeds back into `build-gate/`.

**What it costs.** Some work proven in the workshop will be re-authored (or carefully lifted) into the reviewed package when it's time to actually extend `daedalus.mjs` — nothing is saved on that final authorization step. What is saved is the enormous, demonstrated-in-this-repo sunk cost of specifying every edge case (including, in the family-lifecycle track, OS-level `execveat`/sealed-`memfd` process-identity fencing and Windows Job-Object semantics) for a design that has not executed once.

**What breaks if it is wrong.** If the workshop's approach needs substantial rearchitecture once real production constraints (signing, disk-truth re-derivation, Eye authority binding) are applied, the workshop investment (731+903 lines) is written off — but that is the bounded, named cost of a prototype, not a stalled multi-day committee process that also produced nothing to show for it.

### Does the refusal reasoning generalize to "extend in place" as a mode?

The task asks directly whether the family-lifecycle refusal generalizes to extend-in-place for `daedalus.mjs`/the orchestrator. The honest answer is: partially, and not as cleanly as an earlier draft of this packet claimed. Nothing in codex's four rounds of hard stops was about disagreeing with the governance goal (friction, hash, DAG, no-cap pass-or-return, Eye authority) — every refusal was that the *specification itself*, at the rigor this mode demands, kept discovering internal contradictions faster than it could resolve them: an unsound hash definition, an unauthenticated Eye-authority check, recovery semantics that can't represent their own failure mode, tests mandated against interfaces that structurally cannot satisfy them, and finally a credential detector that rejects the very document requiring it. That is real evidence that *fully specifying a signed multi-model lifecycle engine, in one pass, before any of it runs, on top of a from-scratch trust-primitive stack* is a mode prone to this failure shape.

But it does not follow — and this packet should not have asserted — that a mega-plan of comparable rigor aimed at `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` "should be expected to hit the identical wall, on the identical files the merge gate actually calls." That specific claim is contradicted by a precedent on those identical files: commit `2e530b2` landed comparable dual-seat friction machinery (parallel authorship; convergence/conflict routing to The Eye) into `daedalus.mjs` and `proposal-orchestrator.mjs` via an ordinary PR (#99), and `df65e8e` then closed five fail-open gaps The Eye's cold read found — through a normal review pass, not a 5-model authorization council, and by composing the *existing* `runParallelDaedalus`/ledger primitives rather than inventing a new trust-primitive stack. `build-gate/daedalus.mjs` and `proposal-orchestrator.mjs` are indeed strictly more trust-critical than the standalone `multi-model-seats` plugin the family-lifecycle plan targeted (`CLAUDE.md:129-135`) — but trust-criticality alone did not force the heavyweight committee-authorization mode there, and an ordinary-scale extend-in-place succeeded on these exact files.

The more defensible generalization is narrower than originally claimed: the failure shape that recurred four times in `authz-009`–`authz-012` is what happens when a plan (a) is fully specified end-to-end before any of it runs, **and** (b) builds its own new trust-primitive stack instead of composing the reviewed one that already exists. Extension work against `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` can avoid (b) by construction — the whole point of extending in place is that the hashing, signing, and DAG machinery is already there to reuse, which is precisely what `2e530b2`/`df65e8e` did and precisely what the family-lifecycle plan's standalone target package did not have available. That narrows, without eliminating, the risk this section originally claimed would generalize with more force: the case-against-Option-A evidence still applies to a *fully-specified, committee-authorized-before-any-code-runs* mega-plan against these files, but not to extend-in-place as such — for which this repo's actual precedent on these files is a success, not the family-lifecycle failure.

### Recommended ruling

**Workshop-first proof (Option B) for the Stage 0/2/4 delta, using the pattern `docs/runs/production-profile-compiler-1-workshop/daedalus-v2.mjs` already demonstrates — compose around the existing `runParallelDaedalus`/`runWorkshop` join via an injected join function, never modify the shipped join — followed by a scoped, single-stage extension against `build-gate/daedalus.mjs` + `proposal-orchestrator.mjs`, reviewed at the ordinary PR-and-cold-review weight `2e530b2`/`df65e8e` already established on these exact files, once a stage's mechanics have a real passing test suite in a disposable location.**

This is a narrower recommendation than an earlier draft of this packet made, and it rests on weaker grounds than "this repo ran a clean natural experiment and the evidence is univocal" — that framing does not survive scrutiny. Two corrections changed the reasoning:

1. The family-lifecycle plan's four refusals are confounded by scope, not just by mode: its Task list shows it built an entire new trust-primitive stack from scratch (hashing, signed DAG store, controller/CLI, replay/handoff), never reusing TELOS's reviewed `merkle-dag`/`build-gate` infrastructure. A `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` extension does not have to repeat that mistake — the existing hashing, signing, and DAG machinery is already there to compose around, which removes one of the two conditions (reinventing trust primitives) that the refusal evidence actually indicts.
2. There is a direct, on-point counter-precedent on these exact files: `2e530b2`/`df65e8e` landed and hardened comparable dual-seat friction machinery in `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` through an ordinary PR and a normal cold-review pass — not a 5-model Eye-authorization council — and it succeeded. That means a full committee-authorized mega-plan (Option A as originally framed) is not shown to be *necessary* for extending these files; it is only shown, by the family-lifecycle evidence, to be risky when combined with fully specifying an entire new trust stack in one pass before anything runs.

What still independently supports workshop-first for the Stage 0/2/4 mechanics themselves, regardless of how the "mode of proceeding" question above is ultimately resolved: the workshop already exists, already passes 35/35 tests, cost zero council rounds, and left the shipped orchestrator untouched. There is no evidence against using it, only evidence that the reason originally given for using it (a clean mode-of-proceeding natural experiment) overstated its case.

What this packet cannot honestly settle, and flags for The Eye rather than asserting: whether the eventual scoped extension of `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` needs full authorization-council treatment at all, or whether the `2e530b2`/`df65e8e` precedent — ordinary PR plus cold review, no mega-plan, no council — is the actually-proven and preferable mode for these files specifically. This packet's evidence leans toward the latter (it is what already worked, on these files, on comparable machinery) but does not prove it generalizes to the full Stage 0/2/4 scope, which is larger than what `2e530b2`/`df65e8e` shipped.

What would flip this: if The Eye rules that `daedalus.mjs`/`proposal-orchestrator.mjs` may never receive code that did not originate inside a reviewed, signed, Eye-authorized package — i.e., that workshop prototypes are categorically inadmissible as design evidence no matter how well-tested, and that `2e530b2`/`df65e8e`'s ordinary-review precedent should not be relied on for future extensions of this size — then Option A's committee-authorized discipline becomes the only legal path, and the fix is to *shrink* the family-lifecycle plan's scope (split its 11 tasks into independently authorizable slices, so a single credential-detector self-contradiction doesn't block the whole plan) rather than to keep prototyping around it. It also flips per-stage rather than globally: Stage 0 and Stage 2 already have a passing workshop proof, so for those two stages the workshop step is arguably already discharged and the live question is promotion, not "which path" — while Stage 3/4 (code produce/challenge) has no workshop evidence at all yet and remains open regardless of how item 6 is ruled.
